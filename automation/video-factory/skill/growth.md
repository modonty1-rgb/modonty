# Growth playbook — reach, subscribers, and what "going viral" actually is

Read this as: what YouTube says it ranks on, what that means for how we build each video, and the
actions after publishing. Sources at the bottom; where a point is third-party and not YouTube's own,
it says so.

## What the Shorts feed ranks on (YouTube's own words)

YouTube says Shorts are ranked on performance and viewer personalisation. Performance means the
share of viewers who **chose to view** rather than swipe, **average view duration** and **average
percentage viewed**; likes and post-watch surveys measure enjoyment. Your Short can get fewer
impressions when other channels' Shorts are doing better. There is no minimum posting cadence.

So "viral" is not a trick. It is a Short that keeps winning the swipe test against everything else
shown to the same viewer, so the feed keeps showing it to more people. Everything below serves that.

## Building for the swipe test

1. **The first second decides.** Picture moving at frame one, hook on screen and in the voice. A logo
   intro is a swipe.
2. **Finish rate over length.** 25–40 s that people finish beats 58 s they leave.
3. **The loop.** A last line that flows into the first gets rewatches, which raise average % viewed.
4. **Topics people already want.** Pick articles that match what the Content Radar on the server
   is seeing this week (trends, seasonal, sector news). Timely beats evergreen in the feed.
5. **Audio Library music** — YouTube says using its sounds may surface a Short on that sound's page.

## Where subscribers come from

- **Long videos convert more subscribers than Shorts.** Third-party: a 2023 Galloway/Gileta analysis
  of 5,400 Shorts across 33 channels found long-form averaged more subscribers per 10,000 views. We
  only read secondary write-ups of it, not the study itself. Treat Shorts as the reach and the weekly
  long video as the subscriber engine.
- **Related Video on every Short** → the regular video it was cut from (`long.md`) (YouTube's own recommendation, with a
  verbal or visual call in the last 5 seconds, and the long video paying off the promise in its first
  5–10 seconds).
- **Series people recognise.** Third-party (Galloway, 2026): people overestimate the audience's
  appetite for novelty and underestimate its appetite for more of what it already likes; a format
  at 2x your average views is a signal, twice in a row is a winner. That is why the five series in
  `formats.md` are fixed and the content inside them changes.
- **Channel page as a menu:** one playlist per series, a trailer, sections ordered by series.

## After every publish — the actions

| action | why |
|---|---|
| Set Related Video to the anchor it was cut from | YouTube's documented path from Shorts to long-form |
| Pin a comment with a question | comments are content ideas and engagement |
| Reply to the best comment **with a Short** (Studio: Reply → camera) | the commenter is notified and the reply shows in the comment feed (YouTube) |
| Add a Poll or Quiz sticker on «صح ولا خطأ؟» Shorts | low-friction interaction (YouTube) |
| Send the video file to the client to repost on their accounts | each clinic has its own audience; the Modonty logo travels with it |
| Post to Modonty's WhatsApp and Telegram channels | the server already posts there daily |
| Embed the video in its article (phase 4) | the article page sends viewers to the channel |

## Measuring — every Sunday with `yt-retention` and `yt-plan`

| metric | where | decision |
|---|---|---|
| Viewed vs swiped away | Studio → Content → Shorts | low = the hook; rewrite the hook pattern for that series |
| Average % viewed | Studio | low = cut length or tighten beats |
| Views vs channel average, per series | Studio | 2x twice in a row → that series gets more slots |
| Subscribers per 1,000 views | Studio | which series and which long video build the channel |
| Visits from YouTube | GA4, `utm_source=youtube` | the value we show the client |
